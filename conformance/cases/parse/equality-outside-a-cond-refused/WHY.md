# Why this case exists

The `=` form of `parse/comparison-outside-a-cond-refused`, which is a different
parse and not a duplicate.

§5: "**The split is at the cell's first `=`.** `=` is outside `ident` (§4.1.9),
so the first `=` can only be the separator, and every later one belongs to a
predicate". So this cell's name is `flag` and its formula is `q = 3` — the
second `=` is *inside the formula*, and §5's sentence about later `=`s belonging
to a predicate does not apply, because there is no predicate here.

`q = 3` is not generated by `expr`, so §9.20 refuses the cell. The available
wrong readings are unusually varied: a reader that splits at the *last* `=`
gets a column named `flag = q`, which §9.16 refuses for the space; a reader that
gives `=` a comparison meaning in `expr` gets a boolean column; and a reader
that gives up on the split gets a stored column of blanks. All three are
outcomes §4.2's whole-cell recognition paragraph forbids.
